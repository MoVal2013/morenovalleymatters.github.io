🐾 ANIMAL HELP & RESOURCES

Help for Moreno Valley pet owners and people who find animals.

Lost a pet? Found an animal? Need help with food or veterinary care? Facing a temporary crisis? Dealing with a pet after an owner's death or move to long-term care? Or reached the point where you can no longer keep a pet? There may be resources that can help.

Moreno Valley Matters® does not facilitate individual pet sales, giveaways or rehoming. This page is provided to help residents find organizations and services equipped to assist with animal-related needs.

Services, eligibility, funding and availability can change. Contact each organization directly to confirm current assistance.

🔎 Lost or Found a Pet

Lost a pet

Start with Moreno Valley Animal Services. Check shelter listings frequently and report the animal missing through the appropriate lost-pet services. If the pet is microchipped, immediately confirm that the registry has your current phone number.

Moreno Valley Animal Shelter
14041 Elsworth Street, Moreno Valley
951-413-3790
Moreno Valley Animal Services

Things to do right away:

  • Search the immediate neighborhood and ask neighbors to check garages, sheds, yards and security-camera footage.
  • Post a clear, recent photo with the location and date last seen on local lost-pet and neighborhood networks.
  • Check nearby shelters as well as Moreno Valley; animals do not recognize city boundaries.
  • Keep checking. An animal may not arrive at a shelter immediately.

Found a pet

If it is safe to do so, check for identification and have the animal scanned for a microchip. Veterinarians and shelters commonly provide microchip scans. Report found animals to Moreno Valley Animal Services.

Do not assume an animal that looks dirty, thin or frightened was abandoned. A lost pet can deteriorate quickly.

🌈 When a Pet's Owner Dies or Can No Longer Provide Care

The death, hospitalization or long-term-care placement of an owner can leave family members unexpectedly responsible for an animal. Do not assume immediate shelter surrender is the only option.

  • Ask whether a family member, friend or neighbor who already knows the animal can provide temporary or permanent care.
  • Contact the animal's veterinarian for medical records and possible local resource referrals.
  • Contact the rescue, shelter or responsible breeder from which the animal originally came; some require or encourage animals to be returned to them.
  • Contact an established breed-specific rescue when appropriate.
  • If permanent placement is necessary, use the responsible rehoming resources below.
Plan ahead for pets. Pet owners, particularly people living alone, should consider identifying someone willing to care for their animals if they die or become incapacitated. Keep the pet's veterinarian, medications, microchip information, feeding instructions, medical conditions, behavior information and preferred caregiver information somewhere family can find it.

🏠 If You Can No Longer Keep Your Pet

If keeping the animal is not possible, responsible rehoming is preferable to abandonment. Riverside County Animal Services encourages owners of healthy pets to explore alternatives such as friends and family, direct rehoming and appropriate rescue organizations before shelter relinquishment.

Structured owner-to-owner rehoming
Rehome by Adopt-a-Pet provides a structured way for owners to create a pet profile, communicate with potential adopters and arrange direct placement.

Home To Home is another direct owner-to-owner rehoming platform.

Rescue organizations

Breed-specific and established rescue organizations may sometimes accept animals or assist with placement. Intake policies and foster space vary considerably. Always contact the organization before transporting or surrendering an animal.

Riverside County Pet Support Center
If you have an emergency, have exhausted other options and cannot continue holding the animal, contact Riverside County about current options. Owned-animal intake may depend on capacity and urgency.
1-888-636-7387 (PETS)
Riverside County Animal Services
Never abandon an animal. Leaving a pet on the street, in a park, outside a shelter or elsewhere does not give it a better chance. If you cannot safely continue caring for an animal and cannot find an appropriate placement, contact animal services or an established animal-welfare organization for help.

🐇 Rabbits, Birds, Reptiles & Other Domestic Animals

Do not assume every shelter or rescue accepts every species. Call first. Riverside County shelters may handle animals beyond dogs and cats, including rabbits and reptiles. For birds, rabbits, reptiles and other specialty pets, an established species-specific rescue may also be appropriate.

🐴 Livestock & Animals During Disasters

R.E.A.R.S. – Riverside Emergency Animal Rescue System
Riverside County's emergency animal system provides rescue, evacuation, sheltering, care and welfare for domestic and livestock animals during disasters and extraordinary emergencies. This is an emergency/disaster program, not a routine livestock-rehoming service.
Riverside County Animal Services

🦉 Injured or Orphaned Wildlife

Wildlife is different from a stray domestic animal. California wildlife officials advise people not to capture, handle, confine, feed or give water to apparently injured or orphaned wildlife without guidance from a trained professional. Young animals that appear abandoned may not actually need rescue.

California Department of Fish and Wildlife
CDFW maintains information about permitted wildlife rehabilitation facilities and what to do when you encounter injured or orphaned wildlife.
California Wildlife Rehabilitation Facilities
Moreno Valley's wild burros
Wild burros should not be treated as ordinary found livestock. Use Moreno Valley Animal Services' current burro guidance and reporting information.
